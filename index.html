Intelligence

One verdict, 18+ independent sources

Every link and sender is cross-checked against live threat queries and downloaded blocklists — never taken on a single provider's word.

phishunt.io PhishTank TweetFeed (URLs & IPs) Phishing.Database DigitalSide Destroylist IPsum Blocklist Project blocklist.de CINS SPF / DMARC / DKIM Certificate & registry data

🔒 phishunt.io, PhishTank, and sender-domain SPF/DMARC/DKIM records are queried live, sending only the URL, IP, or sender domain at stake. A dozen-plus more are downloaded blocklists and URL feeds matched entirely in your browser — your links never reach them. Domain ages are read from each registry's own authoritative RDAP server where one is published, then the rdap.org aggregator; registries that publish no RDAP (e.g. .de) have no registration date available.

↻ Blocklists and feeds refresh on a schedule (every 15 minutes to every 12 hours, per source) and are re-downloaded fresh on every browser start — never stale, never persisted between sessions.

FAQ

Questions, answered

Where does my email actually go?

Nowhere. Email content is analyzed entirely on your device by heuristics and the built-in AI model. The optional Gemini Nano engine, when available in Chrome, also runs on-device inside the browser. Nothing about your mail is ever uploaded for analysis.

What data does leave my browser at all?

Only narrow, keyless requests, and only when relevant: (1) URL or IP checks against public threat feeds — phishunt.io and PhishTank queries live; other blocklists (TweetFeed (URLs & IPs), Phishing.Database, DigitalSide, Destroylist, IPsum, Blocklist Project, blocklist.de, CINS, and more) are downloaded on a schedule and matched entirely in your browser, so they never see your URLs; (2) public domain-registration (RDAP) and certificate-transparency lookups for domain age, from the registries' own public records; (3) a breach lookup to haveibeenpwned.com when you run the breach checker; and (4) a page fetch when you trigger a manual page check. Everything read is publicly available data — never email content, identity, or browsing history. See the Privacy Policy.

Which webmail providers are covered?

18+ providers: Gmail, Outlook (including the new Outlook), Yahoo, Proton, Fastmail, iCloud, Zoho, GMX, Web.de, AOL, Tutanota, Mailfence, Posteo, Yandex, Mail.ru, and more — plus a generic fallback for webmail PhishDiver hasn't met yet. Free covers Gmail; Pro covers every provider.

Which browsers are supported?

Any Chromium browser: Chrome, Brave, and Edge. The ONNX AI engine works everywhere. Gemini Nano adds a bonus layer only where Chrome's on-device AI is available (Chrome 127+ with the feature enabled).

Is this a replacement for antivirus?

No. PhishDiver is an aid to your judgment, not a guarantee — and it shouldn't replace antivirus, a password manager, multi-factor authentication, or your own caution. Use it alongside those.

Why two AI engines?

Two independent reads beat one. The built-in AI classifier is always on; Chrome's Gemini Nano (when available) adds a contextual opinion on intent and social engineering. If Nano is unavailable or disabled, the built-in engine keeps working alone — nothing depends on a cloud.

Does it work offline?

Yes. The built-in AI engine, all heuristics, and downloaded blocklists work entirely offline — no internet connection needed for email analysis. Gemini Nano also runs on-device, but Chrome may need an initial connection to download the model. Live threat-feed queries (phishunt.io, PhishTank) and domain-age lookups (RDAP, certificate transparency) require an internet connection, but the extension degrades gracefully — if those fail, the cached blocklists and local AI still protect you. The core protection never depends on a cloud.

Will it slow down my browser?

Short answer: no for most people. The built-in AI model is lightweight and runs in your browser in negligible time — you will not notice it.

The Gemini Nano engine is heavier. On modern hardware (recent Intel/AMD laptops, Apple Silicon, discrete GPU), it runs with no visible delay. On older or low-end machines, it may cause a brief stutter. If that happens, you can turn Gemini Nano off in Settings — the built-in engine keeps running alone.

Battery: The built-in engine has a negligible battery footprint. Gemini Nano uses more power when active. On laptops, if you notice faster battery drain during long mail sessions, disabling Nano in Settings will help. Memory: The extension uses minimal memory for its core operation. Gemini Nano adds its own memory footprint managed by Chrome's built-in AI infrastructure.

Blocklist downloads happen periodically and are small text files. They are cached in memory only, re-fetched fresh on each browser start, and never written to disk. Background bandwidth usage is minimal.

What happens when I click a dangerous link?

The page is blocked before it loads, and you get a warning screen that explains what the link really does in plain language — with Back to Safety, Allow Once, Always Allow, and Block options.

What's the difference between Free and Pro?

Free protects Gmail with heuristics and URL defense. Pro extends scanning to all 18+ providers, adds both on-device AI engines, attachment/QR/calendar scanning, and every advanced detector — BEC, spoofing, job-offer, reply-chain, vishing, payment-detail changes, homographs, clipboard, credential harvesting, ClickFix, iframes, live page scans, real-destination tracking, threat history, the breach checker, and the full-page Dashboard.

How do I get Pro, and can I cancel?

Choose a plan and subscribe — billing, renewals, and cancellations are all handled by Stripe, and PhishDiver never stores your card details. Subscriptions auto-renew until you cancel on Stripe's hosted pages, and you can cancel any time before the next renewal. Annual plans save 17% versus monthly.

Does a "Safe" verdict mean the email is definitely safe?

No. PhishDiver is an aid to your own judgment, not a guarantee. A safe verdict means no known signals were found — always verify unexpected requests for money, credentials, or sensitive information through a channel you know is real.

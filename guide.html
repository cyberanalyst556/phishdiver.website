1. What PhishDiver does

PhishDiver is an aid to your judgment. It reads the emails in your inbox and the pages you visit, looks for the signals that phishing, scams, and malicious links typically share, and tells you what it found in plain English — Safe, Suspicious, or Dangerous, with the reasons.

Three things make it different: there is no account and there are no API keys needed for the protection itself, and your email is analyzed entirely on your device. Nothing about your mail is ever uploaded for analysis. (Pro is a paid subscription billed through Stripe — it uses a license key for entitlement, but never a data account or API key, and it changes nothing about how your mail is analyzed.) It is not a guarantee of safety — no tool is — but it takes the speed out of a scam, and that is usually the one thing a scam can't survive.

2. Install in three steps

  1. Add PhishDiver to your browser. PhishDiver works in any Chromium browser: Chrome, Brave, or Edge. Install it from the Chrome Web Store like any other extension.
  2. Pin it to your toolbar. Click the puzzle-piece icon in your browser's toolbar, find PhishDiver, and pin it. Pinning makes the popup and its settings one click away and keeps the icon visible when a check runs.
  3. Start browsing. That's it — there is nothing to configure before it works. Open an email, hover over a link, or visit a page. PhishDiver is already watching.

PhishDiver scans Gmail on the free plan automatically. Pro extends scanning to all 18+ providers (Outlook, Yahoo, Proton, Fastmail, iCloud, and more). After upgrading, reload any open email tabs once so Pro scanning applies to them.

3. Your first day

Nothing special is required — but here is what you'll notice working in the background:

  • Email verdicts. Every email you open returns one clear verdict — sender, subject, links, and content all checked in a single pass.
  • Hover previews. Hover over a sender, link, or attachment and a small preview appears while the check runs, then dismisses once you move the mouse. It stays open until the result is ready so you always see the answer.
  • Instant link blocking. Known phishing domains are blocked at the network level before the page even starts loading — no flash, no partial load. Heuristic threats are caught on navigation. Both show a warning screen with Back, Allow Once, Always Allow, and Block options.
  • First-contact sender tracking. Messages from senders you've never received mail from before get flagged with a visible chip — the exact vector scammers rely on.
  • Scam pattern education. When a flag triggers, you see exactly which pattern was detected — not just "suspicious" but the specific rule behind it (wire-transfer urgency, gift-card push, brand impersonation, etc.) with a full explanation.
  • Threat intelligence. Every check is cross-referenced against live threat queries and downloaded blocklists — the "Active threat sources" list in the popup shows their freshness at a glance.

Want less interruption? Every behavior has a switch — see Settings explained below.

4. Reading a verdict

Every check returns one of three verdicts, with the reasons shown in plain English:

  • Safe — no known threat signals were found. This does not mean the email or page has been verified safe; it means nothing suspicious stood out. Always think twice before sending money, credentials, or personal details to anyone who asks, however the message looks.
  • Suspicious — something doesn't add up: a familiar brand behind an unusual address, deadline pressure, a request that goes outside normal channels, or a sender that can't be cryptographically verified. Treat it as a prompt to slow down and check through a channel you know is real.
  • Dangerous — a known-bad link, a forged sender, or a strong combination of scam signals. The page is blocked before it loads, and the warning screen tells you exactly what the link really does.

A "Safe" verdict is a starting point, not a contract. PhishDiver keeps false alarms low and presents every verdict as a prompt for your judgment, not a ruling. If an email makes you uneasy, trust the feeling and verify separately — ideally through a phone number or website you already know is real, not one supplied in the message itself.

When the built-in ONNX classifier flags a message, it also names the scam family it found — credential theft, BEC, gift-card, advance-fee, romance, or prize/giveaway — so a "Dangerous" verdict tells you not just that something's wrong, but what the email is trying to do.

5. When you click a dangerous link

Instead of loading the page, PhishDiver shows a warning screen with the blocked URL and what was found. Known phishing domains are blocked at the network level before the page even starts loading — no flash, no partial load. Heuristic threats are caught on navigation. Either way, you see a warning screen with the blocked URL and what was found. You have four choices:

  • Back to Safety — the safe default. Takes you away from the page.
  • Allow this site once — for the single visit you're already on. Use only if you genuinely need to see the page; the next visit is blocked again.
  • Always allow this domain — adds the domain to your allowed list. The warning won't appear for it again until you remove it.
  • Block this domain permanently — adds the domain to your blocked list, so any link to it is stopped everywhere, in every email and page.

You can review and reverse any of these choices any time in the popup's Lists tab.

6. Manual checks

When you want a closer look at something, PhishDiver gives you on-demand tools:

  • Right-click check. Right-click any link, image, or selection and choose PhishDiver: Check this link. You get a full on-demand scan with a verdict, per-source findings, and a copyable report.
  • Current-page check. Run a full scan on the page you're viewing, including a live read of its actual content and anything it may be hiding.
  • Breach checker. From the popup's Breach tab, check an email address or domain against HaveIBeenPwned's public breach database. Only this lookup leaves your browser — and only because you asked for it.

7. The Dashboard

Click the Dashboard button in the popup header to open a full-page overview of everything PhishDiver is tracking. It gives you a single place to see your protection status at a glance.

The dashboard includes:

  • Overview cards — total threats, warnings, checks, and blocked links. Click any card to expand a detailed breakdown with filtered threat lists, stats, blocked domains, or a timeline chart.
  • Threat Breakdown — threat counts by category (phishing, scam, etc.) with expandable detail panels showing the actual threats in each group.
  • Recent Activity — a collapsible feed of recent threat events, expanded on demand.
  • Session Info — extension version, browser, license tier, and feed status at a glance.
  • Own Addresses & Providers — email addresses PhishDiver has learned for self-spoof detection, grouped by provider.
  • Allowed & Trusted — your allowed domains and trusted senders with one-click removal.
  • System Health — AI engine status (ONNX, Gemini Nano) and protection module states.
  • Threat Sources — live status of each threat feed, showing freshness and last-check times.
  • Quick Actions — check a URL or run a breach lookup directly from the dashboard.
  • CSV Export — download your filtered threat data with date range, type, and severity filters.

Advanced Detection (Pro)

Pro users see five additional sections in the dashboard:

  • First-Contact Sender Tracking — how many unique senders you've seen, how many have been flagged, and recent first-contact matches.
  • Persona Guard — matches where a contact's name was used from an unexpected domain, with full context on the impersonation attempt.
  • Scam Pattern Detection — frequency of each scam pattern type across your threat history, so you can see which categories are most common.
  • Signal Correlation — breakdown of how independent signals combined into verdicts, with correlation category counts and bar charts.
  • Brand Tracking Domains — active monitoring status for major brand-impersonation domains (Walmart, Amazon, Target, etc.).

All data stays on your device. The dashboard reads from the same local storage the extension uses — nothing is uploaded or shared.

8. Settings explained

Everything is controlled from the popup's Settings tab. Here's what each switch does:

  • Enable PhishDiver — the master switch. Turns all scanning on or off.
  • Hover tooltips — shows the preview popup on senders, links, and attachments. It stays open until the check finishes so results always appear, then dismisses on mouse move or after stillness.
  • Click interceptor — blocks dangerous pages before they load. Turn it off and dangerous links still get flagged, but the page isn't stopped first.
  • Email scanning — analyzes the full email when you open it. Off means only links and images are checked, not the full message.
  • Gemini Nano AI — Google's free AI that's built into Chrome on some computers. It gives your email a second, on-device read of the sender's intent. Turn it off to use the built-in ONNX AI engine alone. On browsers without it (Brave, Edge, older Chrome), ONNX protects you regardless, and nothing is sent to Google.
  • Right-click check — adds the PhishDiver entry to your right-click menu.
  • Reset counts on browser start — clears the link statistics in the popup every time the browser restarts.

9. Blocked, allowed & trusted lists

The popup's Lists tab shows three lists you control:

  • Blocked domains — always stopped, wherever a link to them appears.
  • Allowed domains — always allowed; no warning is shown for them.
  • Trusted senders — senders you've confirmed you trust, so their messages don't re-trigger alerts.

These lists live entirely on your device. Add or remove domains any time; changes take effect immediately.

11. Troubleshooting

No hover preview appears

Make sure Hover tooltips are on in Settings. The preview dismisses on mouse movement — keep the cursor still over the item for a moment. If it still doesn't appear, reload the page once.

Nothing seems to happen on an email

Make sure Email scanning is on, then reload the email tab. On the free plan, only Gmail is scanned; other providers need Pro. After upgrading, reload open tabs once.

Gemini Nano isn't active

Gemini Nano is a free AI that comes with Chrome — PhishDiver doesn't install it. On computers where Chrome hasn't switched it on yet, here's how to enable it:

  1. Go to chrome://flags and enable Prompt API for Gemini Nano, then relaunch.
  2. Go to chrome://components and update the Optimization Guide On Device Model.
  3. Open the PhishDiver popup and use the Download Gemini Nano model button if it's shown.

The built-in AI keeps protecting your email throughout, so there's no gap while you set this up. If you'd rather not use Gemini at all, just leave it off — protection, and your privacy, are exactly the same with the built-in engine.

My browser blocked PhishDiver

Chrome sometimes disables extensions it can't verify. Re-enable it from chrome://extensions. If you're running a development copy rather than the Web Store build, expect this — it's the browser protecting you.

The icon shows an odd number

Those are live counters, not errors. The popup breaks down what was scanned, warned, and blocked.

12. Your privacy

Your email is analyzed on your device. The only data that leaves your browser is narrow and keyless — and only when relevant: a URL or IP checked against public threat feeds, public domain-registration and certificate records, a breach lookup when you run it, and a page fetch when you trigger a manual page check. Everything read is publicly available data — never your email content, identity, or browsing history.

There are no accounts, no API keys, and no trackers for the protection itself — the only optional exception is the Pro license key used to unlock the paid tier, which is issued by Stripe and verifies entitlement only. Blocklists download on a schedule and are matched entirely in your browser, so they never see your URLs. Full details are in the Privacy Policy.

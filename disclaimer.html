Legal Disclaimers

Effective date: August 14, 2026 · Last updated: August 17, 2026
Read this before relying on PhishDiver. The following disclaimers are part of the EULA and apply to your use of the extension.

1. Not proactive threat protection

PhishDiver is an aid to help you stay alert and vigilant — it is not proactive threat protection, and it does not guarantee your safety. It analyzes email and page content using local heuristics and on-device AI models to surface signals commonly associated with phishing, scams, and malicious links. No detection system, automated or human, catches every threat, and sophisticated or novel attacks may not trigger a warning at all.

2. "Safe" means "no known signals found"

A "Safe" verdict means no known signals were found — it does not mean an email or page has been verified safe, and it should never replace your own judgment. Always independently verify unexpected requests for money, credentials, or sensitive information, ideally through a phone number or website you already know is real — not one supplied in the message itself.

3. False negatives and false positives

No security tool is perfect. A real threat may be missed (a false negative), and safe content may occasionally be flagged as suspicious (a false positive). PhishDiver is designed to keep false alarms low and to present every verdict as a prompt for your judgment rather than a ruling. You are responsible for decisions you make based on its output.

4. Not a replacement for security software

This extension does not replace antivirus software, a password manager, multi-factor authentication, or your own good judgment. Treat it as one layer in a broader security practice, not the only one. Always keep your operating system, browser, and other security software up to date.

5. Third-party services

The extension relies on third-party services that are outside our control, including public threat feeds (phishunt.io, PhishTank, TweetFeed (URLs & IPs), Phishing.Database, Destroylist, IPsum, blocklist.de, aggregate IP blocklists, and more), HaveIBeenPwned for breach lookups, public DNS and registry services (registry RDAP servers, and the rdap.org aggregator), and — where available — Chrome's on-device Gemini Nano. These services may be unavailable, delayed, or incomplete, and each operates under its own terms and privacy policy. We are not responsible for the accuracy, availability, or security of third-party services, or for anything you do with data obtained through them.

Nothing private is ever sent to, or read from, a third-party service.

9. AI model & data sources

The on-device AI classifier was trained on publicly available phishing, BEC, scam, and legitimate email and web page corpora. Datasets used include the Jose Nazario phishing corpus, the MeAJOR corpus, the Zero-Day Phishing Emails Corpus (Mutahir Shaukat), the Enron Email Dataset, the Cross-model LLM Phishing Corpus (Gutierrez et al. 2026), and additional first-party synthetic data built to cover modern scam and business email compromise patterns. Full attribution, dataset names, creators, licenses, and sources are listed on the Open-Source Licenses page.

The classifier itself is a machine-learning model (TF-IDF feature extraction with a logistic-regression classifier exported to ONNX Runtime Web). It runs entirely in your browser via WebAssembly and does not phone home. Runtime false-positive and false-negative rates are reported on the FAQ page.

Training data is re-processed periodically as new corpora are released or curated. The model is retrained on a rolling window; no user email content or page content is ever used for training.
